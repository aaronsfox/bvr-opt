Keyframe-corrected stepwise tracking

How the proposed method turns a rater's keyframes and the biplane radiographs into a pose for every bone in every frame. Each stage starts from the previous stage's result. The tibia, talus and calcaneus are fitted together throughout, so their DRRs overlap correctly and the contact term keeps them apart.

Keyframe Keyframe as given by the rater Forward chain Backward chain Starting path Fitted path
Input

Biplane radiographs

Two calibrated views per frame, N frames per trial.

Input

CT bone models

Tibia, talus and calcaneus volumes. Rendered as DRRs, and as signed distance fields for contact.

Input

Rater keyframes

Poses every s frames, with bookends at frame 5 and N − 6. These come from the user and may be off.

1

Keyframe correction

once per keyframe · one job per trial

Each given keyframe is refitted to the images of the frames around it. Only the corrected keyframe is kept.

  • Over the window, each bone's pose is the given path times a smooth increment: Δ(f) = δ + s·v + s²·w, s = (f − k)/2
  • δ is the keyframe's own correction. It is capped smoothly at the tolerance, with a weak prior keeping it small.
  • Steps are a fixed size in mm and degrees, so a wide tolerance does not mean larger steps.
window
±2 frames
tolerance
1.5 mm / 2° at 1× · 3 mm / 3° at 2× · 6 mm / 6° at 4× (ideal keyframes: 1 mm / 2°)
objective
DRR + frame-to-frame + contact
schedule
256 px, 40 iterations → 512 px, 50 iterations
2

Stepwise chains

per gap between keyframes a and b · corrected keyframes held fixed

From each keyframe, step one frame at a time into the gap, so every frame starts within about 1–2 mm of where it should be. That's inside the range the fit can recover from, unlike a spline start across a long gap.

  • Each frame starts from a constant-velocity prediction from the two frames before it: M₁ · M₂⁻¹ · M₁
  • That frame is fitted on its own, linked by the frame-to-frame term to the frame just fitted.
  • The forward and backward chains are blended frame by frame: rotation along the geodesic between them, pivot position linearly.
objective
DRR + frame-to-frame (previous frame) + contact
schedule
256 px, 20 iterations → 512 px, 20 iterations, per frame
3

Anchored refinement

per gap · keyframes are hard anchors

The whole gap is refined at once, starting from the blended chains. This smooths frame-to-frame noise left by the chains while keeping every frame consistent with the images.

  • Each bone's path is the starting path times a sine-series correction that vanishes at both keyframes: Δ(f) = Σₖ cₖ · sin(kπ(f − a)/(b − a))
  • The number of sine terms and frames sampled per iteration scale with gap length, so every frame gets the same flexibility and image data at any spacing.
basis
K = 3 sine terms per 10 frames of gap
sampling
4 frames per 10 frames of gap, each iteration
objective
DRR + frame-to-frame + contact + small penalty on c
schedule
256 px, 40 iterations → 512 px, 50 iterations
Output

Pose of every bone in every frame

Corrected keyframes plus the refined gaps, saved as .npz and as Autoscoper .tra files. Scored against the bead-based reference: pivot error, helical rotation and translation, and frames beyond Welte's 1 mm / 2° standard.

The objective used in every stage

L = 1.0 · DRR + 2.0 · frame-to-frame + 0.01 · contact (+ 0.003 · Σ|c|² in stage 3)

DRR

All three bones are rendered together into one DRR per camera. Each bone scores 1 − signed gradient correlation with the radiograph, inside its own silhouette.

Frame-to-frame

Points on strong CT edges are projected into neighbouring frames with each frame's pose. The same anatomy should land on the same image edges in both frames (NCC of gradient images).

Contact

Surface points of each bone are checked against the other bones' signed distance fields. Penetration beyond 2 mm is penalised, which allows for cartilage and segmentation error.

The comparison arm keyframe_interp is the spline through the given keyframes alone, with no image fitting. new_opt skips stages 1 and 2 and refines from that spline instead.
